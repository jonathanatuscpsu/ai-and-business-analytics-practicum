Recommended Practicum directions for IHG

The first two directions are recommended starting points because they combine visible business value, executive relevance, measurable outcomes, and a clear path to a 15-week student deliverable. The final scope should be refined with IHG feedback before the project is finalized.

Recommended top choices

Direction 01 · Recommended top choice

AI-Enabled Visual Discovery and Location Intelligence

Assess how AI-enabled visual and location analytics could improve property image quality review, room and amenity tagging, interior and exterior visualization, customer-facing 2D/3D discovery, heatmap analysis, and market-location insight generation.

The student team could build a visual intelligence workflow or dashboard that improves property-content quality, supports guest discovery, and shows how image, spatial, and location signals can inform hotel search, merchandising, and decision-making.

Top choice Computer vision Guest experience Heatmap analysis Location analysis
Direction 02 · Recommended top choice

Financial Performance and Investment Prioritization

Assess how financial analytics could help IHG compare revenue performance, cost efficiency, profitability, budget variance, and return on investment across properties, markets, or segments.

The student team could build a property- or market-level dashboard that combines revenue, cost, occupancy, booking-channel, loyalty, and location indicators to identify margin opportunities, explain variance drivers, and prioritize targeted operational improvement or AI-enabled investment.

Top choice Revenue analytics Cost efficiency Profitability Variance analysis Investment prioritization

Expected IHG deliverables and handoff package

For either recommended direction, the student team would deliver a sponsor-ready package that IHG can review internally and use to inform follow-on implementation.

  • Executive summary dashboard: A concise HTML-based dashboard for leadership review, focused on the selected direction and the most important business implications.
  • Technical report: A concise report covering data sources, methods, assumptions, findings, limitations, and implementation considerations.
  • Reproducible materials: Code, scripts, notebooks, the final HTML dashboard, and supporting dataset(s) needed to reproduce or adapt the analysis. Suggested submission formats include .R, .Rmd, .py, .ipynb, or shareable Google Colab links.
Scoping principle: Each direction can be framed as a discovery, prototype, evaluation, or implementation-roadmap project. The final scope should be narrow enough for students to define success metrics, work with available data, test assumptions, and deliver actionable recommendations within the 15-week semester.

Backup option

Direction 03 · Backup option

Internal AI Workflow Efficiency

Assess where internal AI-enabled workflows create avoidable manual effort, cost, or delays, then recommend practical improvements to process design, quality review, and governance.

Enterprise context: This is an important enterprise topic. Technology firms, AI labs, and large enterprises, including Fortune 500 companies, are investing in governance, evaluation, cost controls, and performance monitoring as their use of generative AI scales.

Why this remains a backup option: A strong project would require access to internal usage data, workflow logs, cost assumptions, and governance context. Without those inputs, the work may become too general and less connected to IHG’s customer experience, property performance, or investment priorities.
Recommended position: Treat this as a Phase 2 option once IHG identifies a specific internal workflow and determines what information can be safely shared.

Data readiness and sensitivity options

High-quality data is essential for meaningful and reliable results. The project can be designed around the safest feasible data option for the selected use case and IHG’s confidentiality requirements.

Data principle

Useful output depends on usable data

Some Practicum use cases may require real-world operational, content, workflow, financial, image, or location data. Others can be scoped around synthetic, anonymized, aggregated, or otherwise non-sensitive data when the objective is process design, proof-of-concept development, or feasibility assessment.

Option A

Real-world data

Best when operational accuracy, realistic validation, or property- and market-level decision support is central. This option requires clear data-use boundaries, access controls, and sponsor review.

Option B

Synthetic or anonymized data

Best for sensitive workflows, early prototyping, and proof-of-concept design when real data cannot be shared directly. This option supports method design while limiting exposure of confidential information.

Option C

Non-sensitive content

Best for projects that can use approved public, aggregated, or low-risk content, such as sample documents, image examples, policy materials, or summary-level data. This option helps teams prototype responsibly while reducing privacy and confidentiality concerns.

Option D

Hybrid approach

Best for early-stage or sensitive use cases where design can begin before real data access is finalized. This option starts with synthetic or anonymized data, then uses sponsor feedback to define what real data would be needed for implementation.

Synthetic and anonymized data use

How synthetic or anonymized data can support the Practicum

When real IHG data cannot be shared directly, the student team can still make meaningful progress using sponsor-approved synthetic, anonymized, aggregated, or low-risk data. The goal is not to recreate confidential records, but to preserve enough structure, relationships, and business logic to test the dashboard, workflow, and recommendation framework responsibly.

1. Schema-based synthetic dataIHG can use a field list, variable definitions, example categories, value ranges, and expected business logic to generate mock records that follow the same structure without exposing real property, guest, employee, or financial records.
2. Anonymized or de-identified extractsIHG can remove or mask direct identifiers, such as property names, guest IDs, employee names, exact locations, and transaction-level details. Students can then work with de-identified records or summary-level tables to analyze patterns safely.
3. Aggregation and generalizationSensitive fields can be grouped into broader categories, such as market tiers, region-level geographies, date ranges, occupancy bands, or indexed financial metrics. This protects sensitive values while still supporting trend analysis, segmentation, and dashboard design.
4. Perturbed or normalized valuesNumerical fields can be scaled, indexed, binned, or lightly perturbed so that students can evaluate relationships and workflows without seeing exact revenue, cost, occupancy, or performance values.
5. Public or approved sample dataFor early prototyping, the team can combine public hospitality, geospatial, market, or image examples with sponsor-approved assumptions. This approach is useful for testing the interface, visualizations, and analytical flow before access to real data is finalized.
6. Hybrid validation workflowStudents can build the prototype using synthetic or anonymized data, while IHG validates selected logic internally against real data and shares high-level feedback. This keeps the Practicum feasible while reducing confidentiality risk.

Recommended use: Start with synthetic or anonymized data for early milestones, then use IHG feedback to refine the dashboard, technical report, reproducibility materials, and implementation roadmap. If real data cannot be shared, the final handoff can still include code, notebooks, dashboard files, and run instructions that IHG can execute internally with approved data.

What makes a strong 15-week Practicum project

Given the semester timeline, the strongest Practicum projects are not open-ended research questions. They are SMART business problems with clear boundaries, visible value, measurable outputs, and a realistic handoff path.

S

Specific

Define the exact workflow, decision, user group, or customer experience the project will improve.

M

Measurable

Identify success metrics such as cost, time, accuracy, quality, usability, executive relevance, or adoption readiness.

A

Achievable

Match project ambition to available data, student skills, sponsor access, and the 15-week course timeline.

R

Relevant

Connect the project directly to IHG’s business priorities, AI strategy, or customer experience goals.

T

Time-bound

Define what can be delivered by each milestone and what should remain for a future implementation phase.

Sponsor engagement model

Strong Practicum projects benefit from steady, lightweight sponsor engagement. Jonathan Fan will lead Practicum execution and student-team coordination, while IHG provides context, answers focused questions, clarifies data or business assumptions, and offers timely feedback at key milestones.

Projected IHG leadership and project-team time commitment: approximately 13–21 hours per assigned student team across the semester.
This estimate includes a 1-hour kickoff, weekly 30–60 minute check-ins, two 1-hour milestone feedback sessions, and a 2–3 hour final evaluation. The cadence can be adjusted based on the number of teams, data complexity, and the level of feedback IHG would like to provide between milestones.
Practicum execution lead: Jonathan Fan will oversee Practicum execution, coordinate student-team progress, and manage sponsor feedback throughout the semester.

Kickoff: present the challenge 1 hour

2

3

4

Milestone 2 feedback 1 hour

Review early findings, validate assumptions, clarify data issues, and adjust scope before the team invests heavily in the final build.

5

Review the final recommendations, prototype value, implementation feasibility, limitations, and next-step opportunities.
