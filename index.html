01 / Sponsor challenge

What Regions is trying to solve

Dan Massey's October 9 problem statement identifies a structural tradeoff: commercial AI providers introduce new models and capabilities rapidly, while a bank needs coherent data, access, audit, cost and model-change controls before those capabilities can be made available safely.

↗

Innovation velocity

New models, features and skills arrive faster than internal platforms can be updated.

◎

Control assurance

Data protection, prompt logging, access control and traceability must travel with the experience.

⇄

Unified experience

Duplicating interfaces, training and controls across providers increases complexity.

▤

Operational capacity

Platform changes, vendor tracking and token-cost management compete with other bank priorities.

“

The strategic question: What single, defensible control-platform approach would let Regions benefit from provider innovation faster—while retaining bank-appropriate oversight, traceability, and user experience?

Attribution: Problem framing is based on the October 9–11, 2026 Regions–Georgia Tech correspondence. This is a proposed analytical collaboration, not a vendor recommendation or a claim that any platform meets regulatory requirements.
02 / Proposed roadmap

03 / Illustrative analytical model

Start with use cases, not vendors

The first-semester inventory and risk framework should make differences between low-risk productivity and sensitive, decision-affecting applications visible. The three example tiers below are a discussion aid only; Regions must define and approve its actual taxonomy and release policies.

INVENTORY FIELD 01
Business ownershipLine of business, accountable owner, use-case purpose and status (production / pipeline).
INVENTORY FIELD 02
Data sensitivityApproved classification, confidential information exposure and retention constraints.
INVENTORY FIELD 03
Customer exposureInternal only, employee-reviewed external content, or customer-facing interaction.
INVENTORY FIELD 04
Decision authoritySummarizes, recommends, informs a material decision or initiates an action.
Illustrative · lower-risk profile

Internal productivity assistance

Example: A staff-facing writing or summarization assistant using approved non-sensitive data, with output reviewed before use.

Possible release posture: Faster enablement of new features after lightweight documented checks, allowed-use constraints and appropriate logging. Regions determines all thresholds and exceptions.

Illustrative · elevated review

Human-reviewed business decision support

Example: An analyst assistant that summarizes internal credit-policy materials for a banker; an authorized employee independently verifies and makes decisions.

Possible release posture: Added validation scenarios, data-use checks, human oversight requirements, version tracking and defined approval before enabling changes.

Illustrative · highest scrutiny

Customer-facing or action-initiating workflows

Example: A customer-facing assistant or agent capable of triggering downstream actions. These are hypothetical patterns, not reported Regions deployments.

Possible release posture: Rigorous bank-defined change review, testing, monitoring, access boundaries, escalation / rollback and explicit release authorization.

Important: Risk classes and illustrative release rules are not statements of Regions policy and do not imply that lower-risk uses are exempt from review. The bank determines what controls are necessary for each use case.

04 / Requirements and evidence

Compare control depth with the cost of waiting

Students can develop a requirements-and-evidence rubric that makes vendor discussions more rigorous. The rubric should separate what is asserted in documentation from what is observed in an approved test environment.

CONTROL EVIDENCE

Data handling & redaction

Data boundaries, retention, redaction behavior, approved processing paths and evidence of enforcement.

CONTROL EVIDENCE

Access & audit trail

Roles, identity integration, prompt / response logging rules, auditability and incident traceability.

CHANGE EVIDENCE

Model change management

Version pinning, capability approval, rollback, monitoring, change records and exception handling.

COST / OPERATIONS

FinOps & usability

Token consumption reporting, budgets, usage attribution, user experience and administration effort.

TEST DESIGN

Comparable test scenarios

Common prompts with synthetic data; pass / fail criteria; documented gaps; reproducible test execution.

DISTINCTIVE METRIC

Capability-availability lag

Evidence-based measurement of when released provider features become available and then approved in the bank's chosen path.

Measure innovation lag explicitly

An important differentiator is measuring this lag: vendor marketing rarely makes the time-to-availability tradeoff easy to quantify. Define the dates, validate their sources and report missing observations transparently.

TIMEPOINT 01Provider release

Document public availability date for the defined capability and model version.

TIMEPOINT 02Platform availability

Record first verified availability within the evaluated platform and approved sandbox configuration.

TIMEPOINT 03Bank-authorized availability

If Regions permits, distinguish platform support from the date internal reviewers approve use.

Two separate measures: Provider → Platform lag = platform availability − provider release; Platform → Bank authorization lag = bank approval − platform availability. Missing or unverifiable dates remain unknown, not zero.

The dashboard displays no vendor rankings, measured results or claimed bank performance. Such findings would require Semester 2 evidence and approval.

05 / Accountability and safe data

A clear student–bank boundary

A core boundary for the proposed Practicum is that the student team informs the decision, but the bank and its accountable parties determine and own the controls. Project data and tooling must meet Regions' information-handling rules.

Scope safeguard

Evidence for leaders—not examiner-facing infrastructure

Outputs are proposed analytical artifacts for Regions review. They are not control attestations, regulatory sign-offs, security certifications, production gateways, or an operational chat interface.

Students may deliver

  • Interview synthesis and proposed inventory schema
  • Illustrative risk tiers with transparent criteria
  • Requirements rubric, test design and vendor-evidence matrix
  • Executive dashboard, technical notes and reproducible materials

Students do not own

  • Bank control mapping or examiner-ready attestations
  • Production control plane or bank-connected integration
  • Regulatory interpretation or compliance determinations
  • Final risk-tier policy, approval, deployment or monitoring
Data option A

Approved metadata

Use-case inventory fields and interview summaries cleared by Regions, without customer-level records.

Data option B

Synthetic examples

Fictitious use cases, prompts and bank-like workflows to illustrate logic without exposing sensitive material.

Data option C

Aggregated information

Business-line summaries, indexed adoption measures and approved high-level control maturity indicators.

Data option D

Bank-internal validation

Regions tests sensitive assumptions within its environment and shares permitted summary feedback.

Default posture: No real customer information, credentials, secrets, confidential prompts, production logs or bank system access unless separately approved and governed. Even synthetic and de-identified data require bank review of residual exposure and permitted tools. The dashboard runs entirely in the browser and does not send the meeting-notes form to a server.

08 / Regulatory framing

Use guidance as context—not a student-built compliance map

The policy landscape reinforces the need for bank-owned governance. The references below are included for context and should be interpreted by Regions' legal, compliance and risk professionals.

FFIEC · CAT sunsetThe FFIEC Cybersecurity Assessment Tool was sunset on August 31, 2025. Read the official notice. ↗ Interagency model risk guidance · SR 26-2April 17, 2026. GenAI and agentic AI fall outside this guidance's stated model definition; banks still need appropriate governance. ↗ NIST AI RMF · Generative AI ProfileVoluntary cross-sector risk-management resource, not a banking compliance certification. ↗
Interpretation guardrail: Being outside SR 26-2's scope does not mean generative or agentic AI is exempt from bank risk management, governance obligations or other applicable law. This dashboard deliberately avoids representing the illustrative rubric as an FFIEC / NIST / regulatory control mapping.
